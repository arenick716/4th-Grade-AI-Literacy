4th Grade AI Literacy Night

Hands-on family learning stations exploring Generative AI, Machine Learning, Media Literacy, and Technology Ethics.

Station 01

The Prompt Architect

Core Concept: Generative AI & Prompt Engineering
Skill Goal: Practice precise procedural communication and understand how AI interprets input.
🎯 Your Goal: Work in pairs to build or draw a hidden creation using clear, step-by-step instructions.
  1. Set Up the Barrier: Place the folder divider between you and your partner. One person is the Prompt Engineer (User), and the other is the AI Generator (Robot).
  2. Create the Secret: The Prompt Engineer secretly builds a small Lego structure or draws a simple image behind the barrier.
  3. Write the Prompt: Write step-by-step instructions on an index card using the formula:
    Subject + Specific Shapes/Sizes + Exact Placement + Details
  4. Run the Code: Pass the card to the AI Generator. The AI must draw or build using only what is written on the card—no speaking or pointing allowed!
  5. Compare: Remove the divider! How close was the AI’s creation to the original design?
💡 What It Teaches: AI text and image generators don't "know" what you are thinking. They follow instructions literally. If your directions are vague, the AI fills in the blanks with its own guesses—which can lead to unexpected or funny errors!
Station 02

Pattern Detective

Core Concept: Machine Learning & Algorithmic Bias
Skill Goal: Understand how AI systems learn from datasets and how human opinions create bias.
🎯 Your Goal: Train an "algorithm" to recognize secret patterns, then discover how missing data or personal opinions change the results.
  1. Assign Roles: One person is the Data Trainer, and one person is the Machine Learning Model.
  2. Set a Secret Rule: The Data Trainer secretly chooses a rule (e.g., "Things with four legs" or "Fun weekend activities").
  3. Load the Training Data:
    • Place 4 matching item cards into Tray A (Positive Training Data).
    • Place 4 non-matching cards into Tray B (Negative Training Data).
  4. Test the Model: The Machine Learning Model inspects the trays and attempts to correctly sort 3 new "test cards" based on the patterns they see.
  5. Spot the Bias: Switch to an opinion-based rule (e.g., "Best foods"). Notice how your partner sorts items differently based on their own preferences!
💡 What It Teaches: Machine learning algorithms do not have personal experiences; they only know what is in their training data. If the data is incomplete, outdated, or based on personal opinions, the AI learns and repeats those exact biases.
Station 03

Spot the Bot!

Core Concept: Media Literacy & AI Hallucinations
Skill Goal: Inspect synthetic media critically using visual evidence and key diagnostic tests.
🎯 Your Goal: Examine gallery images with a magnifying glass, spot AI errors ("hallucinations"), and separate real photos from synthetic ones.
  1. Grab Your Gear: Pick up a magnifying glass and an Investigation Checklist.
  2. Inspect the Gallery: Examine each image using the 3-Point AI Test:
    • 🖐️ Anatomy & Symmetry: Look closely at hands, fingers, teeth, eyes, and background people.
    • 🔤 Text & Logic: Is background text real spelling or scrambled shapes? Do shadows point in the right direction?
    • 👓 Edge Alignment: Do glasses frames, background fences, or clothing patterns melt into each other?
  3. Cast Your Vote: Place a Green Note (Real) or Red Note (AI Generated) next to each picture.
  4. Cite Evidence: Write down at least one specific visual flaw that proved your decision.
💡 What It Teaches: Generative AI models predict patterns rather than understand reality, leading to logical glitches called hallucinations. Never assume online media is real just because it looks realistic at first glance!
Station 04

AI Ethics Council

Core Concept: Digital Ethics, Privacy & Responsible Use
Skill Goal: Evaluate real-world technology scenarios and establish healthy family rules for AI use.
🎯 Your Goal: Debate realistic AI scenarios as a family and create your personalized Family AI Agreement.
  1. Draw a Dilemma Card: Read the scenario card out loud to your group.
    Example: "Is it okay to use AI to generate an essay outline if you write the actual paragraphs yourself?"
  2. Vote: Everyone holds up a vote at the same time:
    👍 Allowed 👎 Not Allowed 🫱 It Depends
  3. Debate: Explain your vote! Discuss how privacy, school rules, and honesty apply to the situation.
  4. Take-Home Agreement: Fill out and sign your Family AI Code of Conduct card to display at home.
💡 What It Teaches: Technology is a tool, and humans are responsible for how it is used. Discussing guidelines around privacy, academic integrity, and permission helps ensure AI is used safely and ethically.
